Leaving your PC on costs more than you think

Streaming 24/7 from OBS means a PC that never sleeps. Here’s the electricity, worked out with numbers you can change.

  • A power cut ends your stream until someone is home to start it again.
  • When the internet drops, YouTube ends the broadcast after about a minute. The next one has a new link, and its likes and viewers start again from zero.
  • Updates restart the PC at night, and the stream doesn’t come back on its own.

    Questions people ask first

    Is a 24/7 live allowed on YouTube?

    Yes. No YouTube rule bans looped or pre-recorded lives. YouTube does ask that content isn’t repetitive or mass-produced, so give your stream variety: several videos shuffled, with now playing on screen. Loops and YouTube’s rules

    Will my channel get monetised?

    We can’t promise that, and nobody honestly can: YouTube decides, by its own rules. A stream that stays up keeps collecting watch time; what you stream decides the rest. What YouTube looks for

    Can I use any music?

    Use music you made, or music licensed for live streams. A copyrighted song can get your live interrupted, even with a licence, unless the owner has cleared your channel. No tool can tell you in advance whether a song will be claimed. Music on your live

    Do I need to keep a PC on?

    No. Your videos are on our servers and stream from there, so you can close your laptop. For a phone stream, your phone sends the camera and we add the rest.

    What if my phone signal drops?

    Viewers see your Be right back screen with music instead of the stream ending, and you’re back as soon as your phone reconnects. Go live from your phone

    How do I pay?

    By UPI or card, in rupees, with a GST invoice for every payment. The free plan needs no card.
